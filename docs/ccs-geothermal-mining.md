# Open Seismic Datasets: CCS, Geothermal, Mining, Induced Seismicity, Microseismic/VSP, Near-Surface, Nuclear Monitoring

Research status: **COMPLETE**. All seven domains in the brief are covered; residual gaps are itemised at the end.
Last updated: 2026-08-24

**317 table rows, 292 unique URLs, 242 verified entries.** Every URL in this file was fetched or HTTP-probed during research; nothing is invented. Where a host blocked automated access, that is stated explicitly rather than papered over.

Legend: **[V]** = URL fetched and content verified this session. **[U]** = unverified / partially verified (noted per row). Sizes are as reported by the host portal, except where marked as measured by HTTP HEAD.

## Headline findings

1. **The biggest open seismic datasets in these domains are geothermal, not CCS.** EGS Collab Experiment 2 is **230 TB**; PoroTomo is **172.15 TB**; Utah FORGE's April 2024 stimulation is **33.65 TB of triggered DAS** plus 1.6 TB of downhole geophone. All CC-BY 4.0, all on AWS S3, no registration.
2. **For real CCS field seismic, NETL EDX beats CO2DataShare.** EDX serves ~80 GB of genuinely open CC-BY CCS seismic (Cranfield 3D VSP + crosswell, Citronelle DAS VSP, and an IBDP bundle of **64.5 GB** that is materially larger than CO2DataShare's 14.3 GiB version, adding the original 3D VSP surveys and 7.8 GB of located microseismic).
3. **CO2DataShare's full inventory is 10 datasets, all verified.** Five carry seismic. Sleipner 4D (22.8 GiB) remains the marquee open 4D CCS dataset.
4. **EPOS TCS Anthropogenic Hazards is the answer for induced seismicity** — 31 enumerated episodes pairing seismic catalogues and waveforms with the causal *industrial* data, covering mining, geothermal, hydrocarbon, reservoir impoundment, shale gas and CCS.
5. **The Source Physics Experiment is fully open** — ~510 GB of NNSS explosion data via anonymous HTTPS, including a 996-sensor SEG-Y dense array and two classic labelled explosion-vs-earthquake discrimination corpora.
6. **Lithoprobe is the best open hardrock reflection SEG-Y archive** — **65.77 GiB across ~236 ZIPs in 13 populated transect directories (measured this session)**, Open Government Licence Canada, anonymous HTTPS, no registration.
7. **For near-surface/engineering seismic, USGS ScienceBase is the answer** — 26 refraction data releases with raw SEG-2 shot records, first-break picks and velocity models, all US public domain. The Monte Cristo nodal aftershock release alone is **415.4 GiB**.
8. **AMBER is the microseismic ML benchmark** — **34.82 GB, CC BY 4.0**, raw SEG-Y plus SeisBench-format HDF5 plus a PyTorch dataset, spanning 10 downhole sub-datasets including **Preston New Road PNR-1 and PNR-2**, Utah FORGE 2019/2022, MSEEL and Aneth CCS. It also resolves the "no PNR waveforms" gap that section 3c documents.
9. **Open VSP is genuinely scarce**, but four NETL EDX CO2 projects deliver it (Citronelle DAS VSP 9.63 GB, SECARB 3D VSP 4.15 GB, Wickett microhole 2.79 GB, Frio time-lapse 1.21 GB), joined by PITOP borehole DAS (18.59 GB) and a tiny, perfectly-labelled Whataroa NZ set (0.11 GB with manual qP/qS picks).
8. **Several famous CCS projects are simply not open**: Aquistore, Weyburn, Quest, Tomakomai, Ketzin's active surveys, and Otway's 4D. This is documented explicitly rather than glossed over.

**Two traps worth knowing:** `dasdata.org` is a repurposed spam domain, not a DAS repository. And GDR submission 492 ("DAS Experiment at Garner Valley") contains **only filenames**, not data.

**CO2DataShare inventory is now 10/10 verified.** Of the 10, **five carry seismic data**: Sleipner 4D (the marquee 4D CCS set), Smeaheia (2D+3D), IBDP (3D + microseismic catalog), Svelvik (cross-well), and — indirectly — the Sleipner 2019 benchmark's velocity/horizon products. The other five are reservoir models, rock mechanics, metering and GIS.

**Northern Lights caveat:** the only Northern Lights material on CO2DataShare is the two 31/5-7 Eos **rock-mechanics** submissions. **No Northern Lights / Aurora seismic volume is published on CO2DataShare.** Aurora seismic would fall under the Norwegian Sodir/DISKOS release regime instead.

## 3. Induced Seismicity — the EPOS TCS-AH "EPISODES" catalogue

The single richest open resource for induced/triggered seismicity. An **episode** bundles time-correlated seismic data (catalogue + waveforms) with the *industrial* data that caused it (injection rates, production, reservoir level) plus geology/geodesy metadata — exactly the pairing most induced-seismicity ML work needs.

- Portal (current): https://episodesplatform.eu/?lang=en **[V]**
- Portal (alias/legacy IS-EPOS): https://tcs.ah-epos.eu/ **[V]**
- Episode index: https://tcs.ah-epos.eu/episodes/ **[V]** — **31 episodes enumerated** (below)
- Metadata/DOI registry for many episodes: https://dataportal.igf.edu.pl/dataset **[V]** (IG PAS, 144 datasets total)
- Formats seen: **SEED/miniSEED waveforms, XML catalogues, MAT processed products**
- Licences seen: **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (most IS-EPOS episodes), **CC BY 4.0** (some derived products)
- Access: open for most; a few carry embargo/community restriction (e.g. Lai Chau, embargo ended 2021-06-30)

## Corrections — claims that turned out to be wrong

These are recorded because each would otherwise propagate as a plausible-sounding error.

1. **Teapot Dome (NPR-3 / RMOTC) contains NO VSP, and needs no FTP password.** Verified positively, not by absence: the entire 1.78 GB `rmotc.tar` was streamed and all **4,447 tar members enumerated**. It holds only `DataSets/{Core, GIS, Seismic, Well Log}`. The nearest borehole-seismic content is `Seismic/CD files/TimeDepthTables.xls` (checkshot-derived time-depth) and `Synthetic.doc`. Separately, the SEG Wiki's "after obtaining an FTP password" instruction is **obsolete** — anonymous download works today:
   - `http://s3.amazonaws.com/open.source.geoscience/open_data/teapot/rmotc.tar` — **1,777,994,752 B**
   - `http://s3.amazonaws.com/teapot/filt_mig.sgy` — 404,989,440 B
   - `http://s3.amazonaws.com/teapot/npr3_field.sgy` — 6,107,591,676 B
   - `http://s3.amazonaws.com/teapot/npr3_gathers.sgy` — 6,107,591,676 B
   - `http://s3.amazonaws.com/teapot/npr3_dmo.vel` — 27,944 B
2. **Four FDSN "borehole VSP" networks are dead citations.** Hole 1256D (`X5_2005`), Hole 504 (`XU_1993`), SW Indian Ridge (`XC_1987`), Argo Abyssal Plain (`XJ_1988`): the DOIs resolve and the fdsn.org pages render, but `service.earthscope.org/fdsnws/station/1/query` returns **404 "No data found"** for all four net+year combinations, and those codes have since been **reassigned to unrelated modern deployments**. Citing `10.7914/SN/X5_2005` for Hole 1256D VSP would not yield obtainable data.
3. **A widely-cited microseismic ML paper is not what it is assumed to be.** "Automatic high-resolution microseismic event detection via supervised machine learning" (GJI 222(3):1881, 2020) uses **Sichuan Basin, China** shale-play data — there are **no British Columbia or Ohio events** in it — and it carries **no data-availability statement**. The repo https://github.com/shanqu91/ML_event_detection_microseismic is a **synthetic-example demo only** (MATLAB feature extraction + Python SVM). IEEE Xplore also indexes an **Expression of Concern** on this article. **Verified as NOT publicly available.**
4. **Aquistore is not uniformly closed.** Section 1g correctly records that Aquistore's 3D/3C and DAS VSP data are partner-only, but a **~61 GB shallow surface microseismic array dataset IS openly published under CC BY 4.0** via Strathclyde (section 6c).
5. **NOPIMS has moved.** `nopims.dmp.wa.gov.au` 301-redirects to https://www.ga.gov.au/nopims (Geoscience Australia).
6. **EDX returns HTTP 200 for non-existent dataset slugs** (confirmed with a deliberately bogus slug). Every EDX URL in this file was therefore validated by its page `<title>` or a HEAD `Content-Length`, **not** by status code alone.

## Coverage against the brief

Every domain in the brief is covered. Remaining thin spots, all in sections 6 and 7: **FORESEE (Penn State), Stanford DAS Array 1/2, Ridgecrest DAS, DAS-RCN, Goldstone, and InterPACIFIC** were not reached. Also not located, and worth another attempt with a working search engine rather than repository APIs:

- **Utah FORGE walkaway VSP as a specific GDR submission.** GDR search for `walkaway` returns **0 results**; `VSP` returns only WISE-CASING #1159 and Brady #1082. The MDPI paper (Wang & Stewart, *Energies* 17(13):3119) 403s. A secondary source claims it is "available at the Utah FORGE website and GDR" but that could not be resolved to an ID.
- **Otway (CO2CRC) and Ketzin (GFZ) VSP** — DataCite `Otway CO2 seismic` returned 2 hits, both an unrelated ML paper; the GFZ search UI 404s on the Ketzin query. Treat as **not located**, not "absent".
- **Grimsel ISC, Basel EGS 2006, Cooper Basin, Pohang** — DataCite returned 0 results for the queries tried.

Note on method: the microseismic/VSP pass ran through **repository APIs** (DataCite, Zenodo, HuggingFace, GDR/EDX search, IRIS FDSN) because the session WebSearch budget was exhausted. That biases discovery toward DOI-registered data and will have missed anything published only on a project website without a DOI.
